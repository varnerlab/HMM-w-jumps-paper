We developed a variance-corrected single-index method that centers and
rescales full-return draws for reuse without counting market variance
twice or fitting separate residual generators
(Fig.~\ref{fig:preservation}; Table~\ref{tab:aggregate}). The method
retained heavy tails and improved distributional fit over centered
naive composition in training and the $2025$ holdout
(Table~\ref{tab:oos_composition}), but did not establish an advantage
over direct residual fitting. Transferring SPY's jump settings
improved temporal fit in training but worsened it in the holdout
(Table~\ref{tab:jump_ablation}), showing that single-asset temporal
gains did not transfer reliably. These findings support reuse for
daily-return comparisons, without establishing calibrated tail
coverage (Table~\ref{tab:var}) or validating broader risk applications.
Independent residual draws omitted cross-asset dependence, and
per-path centering removed residual uncertainty from terminal
cumulative log returns conditional on the market path and the loading
used in composition. Portfolio and cumulative-risk applications therefore require
a joint residual model and a construction that retains cumulative
residual variation.
