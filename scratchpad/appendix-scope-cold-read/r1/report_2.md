## Scope judgment and budget

Yes: a whole-appendix target of at most 30 actual prose paragraphs is plausible. This chunk is a particularly strong place to remove scope, rather than merely shorten sentences. Its 17 nonempty prose paragraphs could become **7 paragraphs, with 8 as a generous ceiling**, and its 3 floats could become **0 standalone floats**. P27, P30, P34, P38, P40, P44, P48, and P50 are empty label blocks, not prose paragraphs.

The current chunk develops almost a separate theoretical paper about the single-asset generator. The main contribution, however, is variance-corrected reuse of fitted generators. Readers need to know what was implemented, what the generator fails to reproduce, and why jump calibration should not be interpreted too strongly. They do not need derivations of a Student-t density, Laplace MLE, multinomial transition MLE, the weighted grand mean, or a binomial expansion of the fourth moment. Those derivations neither establish the composition correction nor independently validate it.

Suggested retained paragraph sequence:

1. **Exact nonstandard generator conventions** (P29, with one sentence from P28): forced-step distribution/counter, independent redraw at every forced step, zero-duration handling, first observation and horizon convention. Refer to the existing algorithm instead of restating all its branches. At most one compact kernel equation if the formal kernel is judged necessary.
2. **Fitted-law qualifications** (essential parts of P31/P32/P35/P36): sample-moment estimators, scale versus standard deviation, fallback qualification, and the resulting SPY variance discrepancy. State the stationary mixture in one line if it is useful. No estimation derivations.
3. **No-jump temporal limitation** (P39): one ACF identity or direct numerical diagnostic; ordinary chain loses magnitude dependence within a few days while observed ACF remains positive. Distinguish individual-state dwell from tail-region persistence.
4. **Jump incidence and conditional sampling** (P41/P42/P43): forced fraction, probability of any episode, conditional forced fraction, and approximate initial law. These are different quantities and must remain visibly different.
5. **What duration changes** (P45/P46): short approximate episode-overlap formula, its restricted assumptions, and the autocorrelation-versus-kurtosis tradeoff. No full covariance derivation or lag-by-lag table.
6. **Calibration limitation** (P47): conditioning on jump paths largely removes information about epsilon; boundary estimates and Monte Carlo sensitivity. Keep this conspicuous.
7. **Comparator reproducibility and qualified emission result** (P51): exact HSMM dwell implementation, unequal state counts, holdout qualification, and controlled Gaussian versus Student-t contrast. If retaining all comparator details makes this unwieldy, split it into two paragraphs, taking the ceiling to 8.

This is a substantive removal of material, not permission to concatenate 17 existing paragraphs into seven enormous ones. Approximately 1,000–1,400 words and a handful of compact result equations would be ample for this support role. Routine probability/statistics algebra should leave the paper; optional analytic diagnostics can remain in repository documentation if desired.

## Required support versus optional detail

**Required:** exact nonstandard simulation conventions; the fact that the Student-t parameter is a scale; generator versus empirical variance distinction; observed no-jump persistence failure; jump-path conditioning and its frequency; approximate stationary initialization; weak identification of epsilon; reproducible HSMM conventions and unequal-state-count limitation; the controlled emission result with holdout qualification.

**Optional:** textbook joint-density/Student-t normalization; Laplace and multinomial MLE derivations; stationary-frequency endpoint algebra; weighted-mean algebra; full fourth-moment expansion; skewness mechanism; fitted-CDF-versus-KS-threshold narrative; multiple equivalent rarity descriptions; complete renewal/covariance proof; the 4,000-path mixture-weight sweep; lag-by-lag approximation checks; matrix/partition/dwell visualizations; repeated scoreboard columns.

Do not retain optional details merely because earlier paragraphs refer to them. Remove or redirect those references as part of the later editorial restructuring. Conversely, do not delete comparator-specific caveats while retaining flattering comparison numbers.
