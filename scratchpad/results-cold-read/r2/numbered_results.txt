
\subsection{Single-asset generator validation}


--- Paragraph results.P1 (results.tex:3) ---
[results.P1.S1] The SPY returns had heavy tails and persistent absolute-return autocorrelation in both the training and holdout windows, providing two targets for generator validation (Fig.
[results.P1.S2] [fig:empirical_motivation]; Table [tab:descriptive_stats]).
[results.P1.S3] Individual raw-return correlations were small, although the Ljung--Box test detected joint serial dependence.
[results.P1.S4] We compared HMM-WJ with seven alternatives on the training window, using $1,000$ paths of $2,766$ trading days (Table [tab:model_comparison]; Fig.
[results.P1.S5] [fig:model_comparison]).
[results.P1.S6] The alternatives were independent bootstrap, Gaussian, and Laplace draws; HMM-NJ; GARCH(1,1) with Gaussian innovations; the GRU benchmark; and the HSMM baseline adapted from Bulla and Bulla.
[results.P1.S7] Both HMM variants had KS pass rates within about one percentage point of the fitted Laplace baseline but came much closer to the observed excess kurtosis, which Laplace underestimated at $3.0$ against $7.7$.
[results.P1.S8] Both also had lower Wasserstein-1 distances than Laplace, whereas Laplace had the lowest Hellinger distance among the fitted generators.
[results.P1.S9] Bootstrap resampling passed every KS test while reusing the observed returns, and Gaussian sampling passed neither distribution test.
[results.P1.S10] The GARCH model had the lowest mean absolute error between the observed and ensemble-average absolute-return autocorrelation functions (ACF-MAE) but rarely passed the distribution tests; the GRU showed the same tradeoff.
[results.P1.S11] The HSMM passed $82%$ of KS tests, underestimated excess kurtosis by more than one third, and had the same ACF-MAE as HMM-NJ despite its flexible state durations.
[results.P1.S12] A controlled emission ablation confirmed that Student-$t$ emissions brought HMM-WJ excess kurtosis closer to the observed value than Normal emissions (Table [tab:emission_hsmm]).
[results.P1.S13] The HMM variants therefore captured heavy tails well, but no fitted generator led on every metric.

[results.P2.S1] Jump episodes improved temporal fit at a small cost in marginal fit in training (Table [tab:model_comparison]; Fig.
[results.P2.S2] [fig:model_comparison]b).

[results.P2.S7] [fig:statistical_validation]).
[results.P2.S8] Across all paths, ACF-MAE fell from $0.059$ for HMM-NJ to $0.053$ for HMM-WJ, with small reductions in KS and AD pass rates.

\subsection{Variance-corrected multi-asset composition}


--- Paragraph results.P4 (results.tex:99) ---
[results.P4.S1] We tested whether hybrid composition preserved generator variance while recovering the calibrated market loading across the $423$ non-market assets of the training universe (Table [tab:aggregate]; Fig.
[results.P4.S2] [fig:preservation]).
[results.P4.S3] The six-method comparison included GARCH(1,1)-$t$ on the $393$ assets with eligible fits.
[results.P4.S4] With observed training SPY as the market path, the index trackers QQQ and SPYG used the $R^2$-preserving branch, and the other $421$ assets, which we call non-tracker assets, used the variance-preserving branch without clipping (Table [tab:branch]).
[results.P4.S5] The two trackers targeted the calibrated $R^2$ rather than the generator variance, so we marked them separately (Fig.
[results.P4.S6] [fig:preservation]).
[results.P4.S7] We paired each composed path with its uncomposed generator draw to measure the ratio of composed to generator variance directly.
[results.P4.S8] Across the non-tracker assets, the median of the per-ticker median ratios was $1.3171$ for naive composition and $1.0001$ for hybrid composition, and the hybrid ticker medians ranged from $0.9933$ to $1.0072$ (Fig.
[results.P4.S9] [fig:preservation]b).
[results.P4.S10] Individual hybrid paths still varied around the target, because the correction assumes zero covariance between the scaled asset draw and the market path, whereas a finite path has a small nonzero sample covariance.
[results.P4.S11] Across non-tracker hybrid paths, the median absolute departure of the variance ratio from one was $1.12%$, and its $95$th percentile was $3.41%$ (Fig.
[results.P4.S12] [fig:preservation]b).
[results.P4.S13] The correlation between generator draws and the observed market path averaged near zero for every asset (Fig.
[results.P4.S14] [fig:cov_diag]), which supported the zero-covariance assumption on average but not on each path.

\subsection{Holdout performance and VaR coverage}


--- Paragraph results.P7 (results.tex:195) ---
[results.P7.S1] To test out-of-sample generalization without refitting or look-ahead bias, we froze the fitted models and SIM parameters before evaluating them against $416$ complete asset histories from $2025$ (Table [tab:oos_composition]).
[results.P7.S2] Hybrid composition reached an $87.0%$ mean cross-ticker KS pass rate, against $83.5%$ for naive composition.
[results.P7.S3] Its median ratio of synthetic to observed $2025$ variance was $0.97$, whereas naive composition inflated that ratio to $1.29$.
[results.P7.S4] The JumpHMM-on-residuals method reached an $86.2%$ KS pass rate and a slightly lower median $W_1$ than hybrid composition.
[results.P7.S5] As in training, these holdout comparisons used each method's native mean treatment, and no common-centering control was run on the holdout.
[results.P7.S6] Every method passed more often in $2025$ than in the full-length training comparison, and the gap between hybrid and naive composition shrank (Tables [tab:aggregate] and [tab:oos_composition]).
[results.P7.S7] For every method, the pass rate against matched-length $249$-day training blocks lay between its full-length training rate and its $2025$ rate.
[results.P7.S8] This ordering was consistent with lower test power in shorter samples contributing to the higher pass rates.
[results.P7.S9] The matched blocks also used simulated rather than observed SPY, so the comparison did not isolate sample length or fully explain the rise in $2025$.
[results.P7.S10] The average pass rates also concealed substantial differences among tickers (Fig.
[results.P7.S11] [fig:oos_composition]).
[results.P7.S12] Most ticker-level rates were high, but every method had a lower tail of weakly fitted tickers.
[results.P7.S13] For hybrid composition, about three quarters of tickers passed more often in $2025$ than against their matched-length training blocks, and about one quarter passed less often (Fig.
[results.P7.S14] [fig:oos_composition]b).
[results.P7.S15] The rise from training blocks to $2025$ therefore did not hold for every asset.

--- Paragraph results.P8 (results.tex:221) ---
[results.P8.S1] To test whether the holdout improvement over naive composition extended to reliable tail thresholds, we estimated per-ticker one-day left-tail Value-at-Risk (VaR) thresholds from $5,000$ separate $249$-day paths and counted exceedances on the observed $2025$ histories (Table [tab:var]).
[results.P8.S2] Doubling the threshold pool from $2,500$ to $5,000$ paths changed every method's mean exceedance rate by less than $0.006$ percentage points at either coverage level (Table [tab:var_convergence]).
[results.P8.S3] The coverage level $\alpha=0.95$ has a nominal exceedance rate of $1-\alpha=5%$ and gave each asset about $12$ expected exceedances.
[results.P8.S4] At that level, hybrid composition had a mean exceedance rate of $5.48%$, closer to nominal than the $4.04%$ of naive composition.
[results.P8.S5] At $\alpha=0.99$, the hybrid rate of $1.12%$ and the naive rate of $0.88%$ lay equally close to the $1%$ nominal rate on opposite sides.
[results.P8.S6] The JumpHMM-on-residuals method had rates of $5.62%$ and $1.16%$, close to hybrid composition, and Gaussian SIM had the largest $99%$ rate at $1.72%$.
[results.P8.S7] The advantage of hybrid over naive composition thus appeared at the $95%$ level but not at the $99%$ level.
[results.P8.S8] Neither comparison established a ranking, because each asset contributed few exceedances, only about $2$--$3$ expected at the $99%$ level, and all assets shared one market period, so their exceedances were not independent.
[results.P8.S9] The hybrid mean rates exceeded nominal at both coverage levels, so its thresholds were slightly too permissive on average, and the descriptive check could not confirm their calibration.
[results.P8.S10] The cross-asset standard deviations of the exceedance rates described how realized rates varied among assets; they did not test miscalibration or validate coverage in other periods.

--- Paragraph results.P12 (results.tex:325) ---

[results.P12.S6] The composer's generator variance was fixed at the target residual variance rather than recomputed from the draw, so the residual scale in Eq.
[results.P12.S7] Equation (eq:r2-rescale) was exactly one.
[results.P12.S8] Median loading and $R^2$ estimates from the composed series were close to their targets.

--- Paragraph results.P13 (results.tex:350) ---
[results.P13.S1] The decline of hybrid fit with calibrated $\beta$ (Table [tab:beta_bucket]; Fig.
[results.P13.S2] [fig:preservation]a) was consistent with a larger market contribution diluting the asset generator's shape.
[results.P13.S3] The market loading ratio $\rho_i$ measures the market's share of the target variance and rises with loading for an asset of given variance.
[results.P13.S4] The scaled draw is a full-return path that already carries the asset's whole shape, and composition adds an independent market term to it.
[results.P13.S5] As $\rho_i$ grows, the market's shape makes up more of the blend, which may help explain the lower KS pass rates at higher loadings; Online Appendix [sec:supp-residual-choice] derives this dilution for kurtosis.
[results.P13.S6] A more volatile market raises $\rho_i$ in the same way and, once $\rho_i$ exceeds $1-f$, activates clipping, which the observed market variance never did.
[results.P13.S7] To measure when clipping activates, we multiplied deviations of the observed training SPY path from its mean by $\gamma\in\{1,2,3\}$, which preserved the market mean and multiplied its standard deviation by $\gamma$.
[results.P13.S8] We then recomposed all $423$ assets with the scaled path's variance in the correction, holding the fitted marginals, intercepts, and calibrated loadings fixed (Table [tab:stress]).
[results.P13.S9] Clipping applied to no composed path at $\gamma=1$ and to $76.4%$ and $95.2%$ of composed paths at $\gamma=2$ and $3$.
[results.P13.S10] Because clipped paths retained their fitted intercepts while the market mean stayed unchanged and nonzero, their means shifted by $(\beta_i^{\eff}-\beta_i)\overline{g}_m$ by construction.
[results.P13.S11] The stress test therefore characterized when clipping activated and how it moved asset means; it did not evaluate fit under the scaled markets.
