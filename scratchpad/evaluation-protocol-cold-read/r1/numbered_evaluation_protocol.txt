--- Paragraph evaluation_protocol.P2 (evaluation_protocol.tex:3) ---
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S1] We evaluated the method on $424$ United States equities and exchange-traded funds from Polygon.io.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S2] Each ticker had $2,767$ daily closing prices from January 2014 through December $2024$, which yielded $2,766$ growth rates; we kept only tickers with all $2,767$ prices.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S3] This complete-case selection conditioned the analysis on assets that survived and retained data coverage throughout the training window.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S4] We used observed SPY as the market path in the training comparison and composed the other $423$ assets.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S5] For each asset, including SPY, we fitted the hidden Markov generator used in the SPY analysis (state partition, counted transitions, and emissions) to the closing-price growth rates (Table [tab:hyperparams]; $r_f=0$).
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S6] We regressed each observed asset on observed SPY to estimate $\alpha_i$, $\beta_i$, $R^2_{i,\real}$, and residual variance.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S7] Across the sample, $\beta_i$ ranged from $0.03$ to $1.79$, and $R^2_{i,\real}$ ranged from $0.001$ to $0.933$ (Figs.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S8] [fig:branch_map] and [fig:r2_distribution]).
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S9] We compared six ways to construct the asset-specific term (Table [tab:aggregate]), which we call the six-method comparison.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S10] In this comparison, we set the jump probability to zero in every generator, so each was an HMM-NJ, to measure composition without per-ticker jump calibration.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S11] The naive method used the centered full-return draw without variance correction.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S12] The Gaussian SIM method used independent Gaussian residuals with variance estimated by OLS.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S13] The hybrid method used Algorithm [alg:hybrid].
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S14] The residual-fit hidden Markov method, labeled JumpHMM-on-residuals in the tables after JumpHMM, the software package that implements our generator, fitted a new HMM-NJ to the OLS residual series after converting the residuals to a synthetic price path by cumulative compounding.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S15] The block-bootstrap method sampled the OLS residual series with the Politis--Romano stationary bootstrap and a mean block length of $50$ days, close to $\sqrt{T}$ for the $2,766$-day training series; the holdout used the same block length.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S16] The GARCH(1,1)-$t$ method fitted a conditional-variance model to each residual series.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S17] These four methods added their residual draws to $\alpha_i+\beta_i g_m$ without centering or rescaling.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S18] We accepted a GARCH fit only if the optimizer converged, the likelihood and coefficients were finite, and the variance intercept was positive.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S19] The ARCH and GARCH coefficients also had to be nonnegative with a sum below one, and the Student-$t$ degrees of freedom had to exceed two.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S20] Finally, the unconditional variance had to be finite and positive, and a trial simulated path had to be finite and nonconstant.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S21] All $423$ optimizations converged; $30$ fits failed the stationarity condition that the ARCH and GARCH coefficients sum below one, leaving $393$ eligible training assets.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S22] We rechecked each stored fit against these criteria before evaluation.

--- Paragraph evaluation_protocol.P3 (evaluation_protocol.tex:46) ---
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S1] For each ticker, the naive and hybrid methods received the same generator draw, which isolated the effect of the variance correction; the other four methods generated their own residual draws.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S2] We did not use the optional cross-sectional rank reorder because the experiment tested each ticker's marginal construction separately.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S3] The naive and hybrid methods force the residual mean to zero over the horizon, while the other four methods do not.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S4] To isolate the effect of this difference in mean constraints, we also compared each method's native path with the same path after subtracting its residual mean over the full training horizon (Table [tab:centering_control]).
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S5] Only the residual mean differed between the two versions of each method; the fitted models, random draws, and market path were the same.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S6] Naive and hybrid paths already had zero residual mean.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S7] The control used $100$ replications per asset and the same $393$ assets, those whose GARCH fits passed the acceptance criteria, for all six methods.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S8] Online Appendix [sec:supp-centering-control] gives the control definition and separates centering effects from model fitting.

--- Paragraph evaluation_protocol.P4 (evaluation_protocol.tex:64) ---
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S1] We evaluated each composed series on factor recovery, distributional fit, and variance preservation.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S2] For factor recovery, a new OLS regression of the composed path on the market path produced the absolute errors $|\hat\alpha-\alpha_i|$, $|\hat\beta-\beta_i|$, and $|\hat R^2-R^2_{i,\real}|$ against the calibrated values; lower values indicate closer recovery.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S3] On the variance-preserving branch, the composed $R^2$ approaches each draw's $\rho_i$, capped at $1-f$, rather than $R^2_{i,\real}$, so for the hybrid method the $R^2$ error mainly measures this designed shift rather than estimation error.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S4] Because the reference is the calibrated loading, the loading error of a clipped asset includes the change made by clipping.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S5] For distributional fit, we tested each composed path against the observed returns of the same asset and reported KS and AD pass rates, Wasserstein-1 distance, excess kurtosis, and the Hill index of the absolute-return tail.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S6] Higher KS and AD pass rates and lower Wasserstein distance indicate a closer match; for kurtosis and the Hill index, values closer to the observed asset's indicate a closer tail match.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S7] The six-method comparison did not score volatility clustering; the jump-enabled comparison did.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S8] For variance preservation of the naive and hybrid methods, which share a generator draw, we used the composed-to-generator variance ratio.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S9] A ratio of one indicates equal variances; the $R^2$-preserving branch does not target this ratio.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S10] Online Appendix [sec:supp-validation] gives the metric definitions.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S11] We ran $100$ replications per ticker with seed $1234$, producing $42,300$ series for each method except GARCH, which produced $39,300$ series across its $393$ eligible assets.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S12] Training summaries pooled these series across tickers.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S13] We archived all $250,800$ training-path scores together with records of the inputs, fitted models, source code, and configuration.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S14] The aggregate, branch, and loading-quartile tables (Tables [tab:aggregate], [tab:branch], and [tab:beta_bucket]) were generated from this archived run, with matching method-level summaries distributed alongside the path scores.

--- Paragraph evaluation_protocol.P5 (evaluation_protocol.tex:96) ---
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S1] For the six-method holdout evaluation, we froze all marginal fits, residual models, and SIM parameters estimated from $2014$--$2024$ (Tables [tab:oos_composition] and [tab:var]).
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S2] We evaluated the methods against the $249$ growth rates observed in $2025$.
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S3] Of the $423$ non-market training assets, $416$ had complete holdout histories; GARCH results covered $386$ because its $30$ non-stationary training fits remained excluded (Table [tab:oos_composition]).
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S4] For each replication, we generated a $249$-day SPY path from the closing-price SPY model with jumps disabled and used the same simulated market path in all asset compositions.
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S5] In this evaluation, the correction used the observed training-period market variance in place of the simulated path variance $\sigma_m^2$ in Eqs.
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S6] Equation (eq:scale)--Equation (eq:r2-rescale); the generator variance was still computed from each asset draw.
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S7] The residual scale therefore budgeted for the training market variance, and variation in the simulated market variance could move the composed variance away from the generator target even when the sample cross-covariance was zero.
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S8] Observed $2025$ SPY and asset returns were used only for scoring.
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S9] We repeated the KS, AD, Wasserstein-1, and kurtosis evaluations with $100$ paths per ticker and measured variance, for all six methods, as the ratio of synthetic to observed $2025$ variance.
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S10] Goodness-of-fit tests have less power on $249$ observations than on $2,766$.
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S11] We therefore also tested the same $249$-day holdout composed paths against a randomly selected contiguous $249$-day block of the asset's observed training returns.
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S12] This length-matched comparison shows how the tests behave at $249$ observations without any change specific to $2025$.
[evaluation_protocol.P5.S13] We first averaged replication-level results within ticker and then summarized across tickers so each asset received equal weight.

--- Paragraph evaluation_protocol.P6 (evaluation_protocol.tex:125) ---
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S1] To isolate each method's one-day tail thresholds from estimation noise in a single short path, we used a separate simulation ensemble for the Value-at-Risk (VaR) evaluation (Table [tab:var]).
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S2] For each asset and method, we pooled the daily log returns (growth rates multiplied by $\Delta t$) from $5,000$ separately constructed $249$-day paths, giving $1,245,000$ observations for the lower-tail quantile at each of the $95%$ and $99%$ coverage levels.
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S3] Each of the $5,000$ replications used one simulated SPY path, shared across assets and methods.
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S4] Every hidden Markov generator started from its fitted stationary state distribution, and centering, scaling, and market variance followed the holdout conventions, including the training-period market variance.
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S5] We fixed one threshold per asset, method, and coverage level before counting exceedances on the observed $2025$ returns.
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S6] Each threshold is therefore an unconditional one-day VaR that does not depend on the simulated state.
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S7] Before holdout scoring, we fixed the ensemble size and a set of smaller pool sizes at which we confirmed that the thresholds had stabilized.
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S8] As a simulation check, an independent ensemble of $1,000$ paths per asset and method compared the simulated probability of falling below each threshold with its nominal level (Online Appendix [sec:supp-var-ensemble]).
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S9] Because the pool mixes all $249$ days of each path, each threshold is a quantile of returns pooled over the days of a $249$-day composed path, not the VaR of any particular day.
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S10] Pooling did not remove the construction's horizon dependence or zero-sum residual constraint.
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S11] We reported mean exceedance rates and cross-ticker standard deviations descriptively.
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S12] We did not run a formal coverage significance test, because simulated days, dependent holdout observations, and assets sharing a market are not independent replications.
[evaluation_protocol.P6.S13] No $2025$ data were used for fitting or threshold calibration.
