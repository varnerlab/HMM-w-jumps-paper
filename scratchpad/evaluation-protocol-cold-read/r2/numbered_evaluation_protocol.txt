--- Paragraph evaluation_protocol.P2 (evaluation_protocol.tex:3) ---
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S1] To test variance correction against alternative residual models, we compared six composition methods on $424$ United States equities and exchange-traded funds from Polygon.io (Table [tab:aggregate]).
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S2] Each ticker had $2,767$ daily closing prices from January $2014$ through December $2024$, yielding $2,766$ growth rates; this complete-case selection conditioned the analysis on survival and data coverage.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S3] We fitted the same hidden Markov generator to each asset, including SPY (Table [tab:hyperparams]; $r_f=0$), and regressed the other $423$ assets on observed SPY to estimate $\alpha_i$, $\beta_i$, $R^2_{i,\real}$, and residual variance.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S4] All generators had jumps disabled to isolate composition from jump calibration.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S5] The naive and hybrid methods shared a centered full-return draw, with variance correction applied only by the hybrid method (Algorithm [alg:hybrid]).
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S6] Four alternatives generated residuals from a Gaussian distribution, a residual-fit hidden Markov model (labeled JumpHMM-on-residuals), a stationary block bootstrap, or a GARCH(1,1)-$t$ model, and added them to $\alpha_i+\beta_i g_m$ without centering or rescaling.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S7] Comparator fitting and eligibility rules are given in Online Appendix [sec:supp-composition-evaluation]; $30$ non-stationary GARCH fits left $393$ eligible assets for that method.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S8] Using observed SPY as the common market path, we ran $100$ replications per asset with seed $1234$ and pooled training scores across tickers.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S9] We measured factor recovery by the absolute OLS errors $|\hat\alpha-\alpha_i|$, $|\hat\beta-\beta_i|$, and $|\hat R^2-R^2_{i,\real}|$, and naive and hybrid variance preservation by the composed-to-generator variance ratio.
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S10] Distributional scores were KS and AD pass rates, Wasserstein-1 distance, excess kurtosis, and the Hill index of absolute returns (Online Appendix [sec:supp-validation]).
[evaluation_protocol.P2.S11] Because only naive and hybrid forced the residual mean to zero, a paired centering control on the common $393$ assets separated this constraint from model fitting (Table [tab:centering_control]; Online Appendix [sec:supp-centering-control]).

--- Paragraph evaluation_protocol.P3 (evaluation_protocol.tex:37) ---
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S1] To test performance without refitting, we froze all $2014$--$2024$ fits and evaluated $100$ simulated paths per asset against the $249$ growth rates observed in $2025$ (Table [tab:oos_composition]).
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S2] Complete holdout histories were available for $416$ assets, including $386$ with accepted GARCH fits.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S3] Each replication used one simulated $249$-day no-jump SPY path shared across asset compositions.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S4] The correction used the observed training market variance in Eqs.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S5] Equation (eq:scale)--Equation (eq:r2-rescale), while each asset's generator variance remained draw-specific.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S6] Consequently, variation in simulated market variance could move composed variance away from the generator target even at zero sample cross-covariance.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S7] Observed $2025$ returns were used only for scoring.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S8] We repeated the KS, AD, Wasserstein-1, and kurtosis evaluations and measured variance for all six methods relative to observed $2025$ variance.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S9] Because goodness-of-fit tests have less power on short samples, we also compared the same simulated paths with randomly selected contiguous $249$-day blocks of observed training returns as a length-matched training reference.
[evaluation_protocol.P3.S10] We averaged replications within ticker before summarizing across tickers, giving each asset equal weight.

--- Paragraph evaluation_protocol.P4 (evaluation_protocol.tex:57) ---
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S1] To estimate one-day Value-at-Risk (VaR) thresholds without relying on a single short path, we pooled daily log returns, $\Delta t$ times the growth rates, from $5,000$ separately composed $249$-day paths per asset and method (Table [tab:var]).
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S2] Each replication shared one simulated SPY path across assets and methods and followed the holdout centering and scaling conventions.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S3] We fixed the $95%$ and $99%$ coverage thresholds before scoring observed $2025$ returns; no holdout data entered fitting or threshold calibration.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S4] These unconditional thresholds describe a uniformly selected day within the $249$-day construction, retain its horizon dependence, and do not remove the zero-sum residual constraint of naive and hybrid paths.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S5] We reported mean exceedance rates and cross-ticker standard deviations descriptively, without treating dependent days or assets sharing a market as independent replications.
[evaluation_protocol.P4.S6] Pool-size convergence and an independent simulation check are reported in Online Appendix [sec:supp-var-ensemble].
