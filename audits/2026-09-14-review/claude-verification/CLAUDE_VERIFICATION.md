## Evidence and verdicts

**R1. Confirmed.** The centering at `Composers.jl:18` subtracts the path's own mean. Lines 106, 121, and 124 scale that centered vector and add it, so every residual path sums to exactly zero. The composed path total is then a deterministic function of the market path and the loading. The probe's terminal standard deviations of order 1e-16 are float rounding of an exact zero, and its Gaussian control value agrees with the arithmetic budget. The scale also uses the whole path's sample variance at `Pipeline.jl:289` and again at line 454, so an initial segment's law depends on the requested horizon. The paper states the exact zero mean in Methods and again in the appendix, and it states that residual-fit methods keep random means. It does not state the loss of cumulative uncertainty.

One consequence the first review missed. In the training comparison the market is observed SPY, and the calibrated intercept satisfies α = Ḡ_i − βḠ_m by `Pipeline.jl:184`. Every naive and hybrid path therefore has a sample mean identical to the real asset's sample mean. Residual-fit paths carry a random location error whose scale is the residual methods' intercept error in Table 3. Table 3's KS, AD, and Wasserstein comparisons are not like-for-like in location, and hybrid benefits. The size of that benefit cannot be read from the packet. In the holdout the market is simulated, so the exact match disappears there.

P1 is proportionate for the text and for the comparison, but the first review's word "problem" should not be read as implementation error. The generator's stationary mean is in closed form in the appendix, so centering by it is a one-line change and the natural fix for a model presented as reusable. That change reruns everything downstream. Disclosure plus an equal-centering comparison is the cheaper compliant route. Which route to take is the authors' decision.
