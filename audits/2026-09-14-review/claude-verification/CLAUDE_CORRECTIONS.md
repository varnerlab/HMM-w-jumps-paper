**2. Universal corrected null. Accepted.** The 1.37 to 1.39 percent figures come from iid uniform, normal, and Student-t demonstrations. The actual synthetic paths are composed HMM samples with serial dependence, path centering, and path scaling, and their sampling distribution differs by asset. The expected exceedance of a type-7 sample quantile at rank 3.48 is only pinned at 3.48 over 250 for iid continuous draws. Under dependence and exact path centering, neither the magnitude nor, strictly, the direction is established from the packet. The first review said the demonstration is not a subtractable correction, and my report contradicted that by calling it the procedure's own null and reading a corrected hybrid excess off it. I withdraw both. The same objection applies to my claim that the hybrid versus JumpHMM-on-residuals comparison survives because both use the same procedure. Sharing a biased procedure does not imply sharing the bias size when the two generators differ in dependence and centering.

The justified conclusion is this. The Kupiec test's nominal null is mismatched to the threshold procedure, the mismatch is plausibly upward and of order tenths of a point, and its size for each fitted generator is unknown. The repair is to estimate the procedure-implied null per asset by simulating many paths from the fitted generator, forming the 249-day threshold as the pipeline does, and evaluating its true exceedance against a large independent sample from the same generator. Alternatively, estimate thresholds from a pooled synthetic sample so the finite-sample bias becomes negligible, then rerun the Kupiec test. Both hybrid and residual-fit rows must be re-evaluated; no ranking is preserved by argument alone.

**5. R1 identity. Accepted; qualification added.** The sample-mean identity holds for naive paths and for unclipped hybrid and tracker paths, where the intercept is the calibrated α and the residual sums to zero. Under clipping, the loading changes while the intercept does not, so the path mean shifts by the loading change times the observed market mean, as R9 states. The reported primary run is described in the packet as unclipped, so the identity applies to the paths behind Table 3 on that description. It is not a property of the implementation in general, and the stress-table rows are excluded.

**6. R11 degeneracy. Accepted.** The two samples share the observed market path but carry independent Gaussian residual draws, so the comparison is not mathematically degenerate. Because the tracker scale is exactly one by construction, the two samples have the same distribution and the null is true. The shared market component induces dependence that an iid-calibrated two-sample KS test does not account for, which can make it conservative. Zero rejections in 1,500 tests at the 5 percent level, against an expectation near 75, is consistent with conservative calibration and with the residual contributing little variance relative to the shared market. The correct characterization is a limited validation that confirms construction rather than recovery, with potentially conservative calibration. It is not a proof of degeneracy. P2 stands.
