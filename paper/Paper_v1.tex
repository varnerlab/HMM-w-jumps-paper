\title{Factor-Informed Cobb-Douglas Portfolio Allocation with Dynamic Risk Aversion}
\author{Jeffrey D. Varner}
\address{Robert Frederick Smith School of Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering, Cornell University, Ithaca, NY 14853}


\begin{abstract}
\begin{linenumbers}
We propose a factor-informed Cobb-Douglas portfolio allocation framework in which asset preference coefficients are derived analytically from Single Index Model (SIM) parameters and updated dynamically through an exponential moving average (EMA) crossover signal.
The preference score $\gamma_{i} = \tanh(\alpha_{i}/\beta_{i}^{\lambda} + \beta_{i}^{1-\lambda}\mathbb{E}[\bar{\mu}_{m}])$ encodes the risk-adjusted expected excess growth of each asset as a bounded quantity in $(-1,1)$, with the sign determining portfolio inclusion or exclusion automatically.
We prove that the resulting Cobb-Douglas utility maximization subject to a budget constraint has a closed-form solution computable in $O(N)$ operations, eliminating any need for a quadratic programming solver.
A daily EMA crossover rule $\lambda_{t} = -G(\bar{S}^{(\circ)}_{t}/\bar{S}^{(\ell)}_{t}-1)$ adjusts the risk-adjustment sensitivity in real time without re-estimating the factor model, translating standard technical momentum signals into a utility-theoretic preference shift.
We evaluate the resulting TradeBot algorithm against passive SPY, SIM minimum variance, equal-weight ($1/N$), fixed-$\lambda$, and risk-free benchmarks over $K=100$ synthetic market paths generated by a 100-state Hidden Markov Model fitted to historical SPY daily returns.
Results show that the dynamic EMA mechanism provides a consistent improvement in Sharpe ratio over the fixed-$\lambda$ ablation, and the closed-form allocation is competitive with or outperforms the QP-based SIM minimum variance strategy across simulated market regimes.
\end{linenumbers}
\end{abstract}
