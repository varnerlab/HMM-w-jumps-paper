This study introduced a hybrid hidden Markov modeling framework to replicate the statistical regularities of empirical equity excess growth rates. By integrating a quantile-discretized state space with a Poisson-driven jump-duration mechanism, we developed a generative framework that captured the leptokurtic distributions and volatility clustering observed in the SPY exchange-traded fund. Our results confirmed that while increasing the state resolution to $N=100$ provided the granularity to characterize the central mass of the excess growth rate distribution, standard Markovian transitions alone did not reproduce higher-order temporal dependencies. The inclusion of discontinuous shocks through a jump-diffusion extension was needed to drive the model into persistent high-volatility regimes, thereby replicating the ARCH effect identified in historical data.

Statistical validation using Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Anderson-Darling tests demonstrated high in-sample consistency, with pass rates of 97.1 percent (SE 0.5) and 90.4 percent (SE 0.9), respectively, across 1,000 simulated paths. Out-of-sample testing on 249 trading days from 2025 yielded pass rates of 95.0 percent (SE 0.7) and 95.4 percent (SE 0.7), respectively. Furthermore, the correlation distance analysis confirmed that our framework produced independent realizations that remained uncorrelated with specific historical timing; this satisfied the requirements of the efficient markets hypothesis and distinguished our generative methodology from predictive forecasting tools \cite{fama_efficient_1970, assefa_generating_2021, kwon_can_2024}. The proposed framework offered an alternative to standard likelihood-based estimation methods by relying on empirical estimation. By bypassing the complexities of the expectation-maximization algorithm through direct frequentist counting, the model remained suited for large-scale stress testing and risk management applications \cite{ohara_market_1998, manganelli_2004, glasserman_monte_2003}. Future research could extend this discrete-state logic to multi-asset portfolios or incorporate foundation models for inference to further refine the detection of Markov jump processes in higher-frequency data \cite{dias_mixture_2010, berghaus_foundation_2025}. Ultimately, the hybrid hidden Markov model served as a foundation for simulating market dynamics and generating synthetic time series that remained statistically consistent with underlying market regimes \cite{nguyen_hidden_2018, hamilton_regime_2018}.

While the current framework was able to generate statitically consistent excess growth rate distributions, there were several limitations that could be addressed in future work. First, the model assumed stationarity in transition dynamics and jump hyperparameters, which may not hold during periods of structural market change. The out-of-sample ACF analysis (Figure~\ref{fig:statistical_validation}, panel~d) provided direct evidence of this limitation: the IS-calibrated $(\epsilon^*,\lambda^*)$ underestimated volatility clustering persistence in the 2025 test window, consistent with the elevated macro uncertainty of that period. Future extensions could incorporate time-varying transition matrices or regime-dependent jump parameters, for example allowing $\epsilon$ and $\lambda$ to evolve as functions of a latent volatility state or observable macro indicators, to capture evolving market conditions. Second, the use of a fixed quantile-based state space may limit the model's ability to adapt to changing volatility regimes; adaptive binning strategies or non-parametric state definitions could enhance flexibility. Third, while the Laplace distribution provided a good fit for excess growth rates, alternative distributions or mixture models could be explored to better capture tail behavior and skewness. Finally, the Single-Index Model extension presented in Section~\ref{sec:results} demonstrates that the HMM-WJ framework can, in principle, scale from a single asset to a full equity universe by coupling the generative SPY engine with an asset-level factor model. The SIM serves this role as a proof of concept: it is intentionally minimal (one factor, linear, symmetric residuals) and consequently inherits well-known limitations; the median in-sample KS pass rate of 66.7\% across 424 assets confirms that a single-factor linear decomposition cannot capture the heterogeneous tail behaviour, skewness, and sector-specific dynamics present in individual equities \cite{sharpe_simplified_1963, fama_french_1993}. Importantly, however, the architecture is modular: the HMM-WJ generative engine for the market factor is decoupled from the factor model used to propagate paths to individual assets. Replacing the SIM with a multi-factor specification (e.g., Fama--French three- or five-factor models \cite{fama_french_1993}), a principal-component-based factor structure, or a nonlinear model such as a neural network residual generator could substantially improve asset-level distributional fidelity without modifying the underlying Markov chain machinery. The out-of-sample SIM results (median KS pass rate of 91.8\% over 249 trading days in 2025) provide a baseline for such extensions and suggest that even a rudimentary factor model, when paired with the HMM-WJ, can produce statistically plausible multi-asset scenarios suitable for portfolio-level stress testing and risk assessment.
