
We simulated 1,000 paths of 2,766 trading days using VWAP price data for both the standard no-jumps model and the hybrid model with jumps. Applying the multi-objective grid search detailed in the Methods, the optimal jump hyperparameters were identified as $\epsilon^* \approx 5 \times 10^{-5}$ and $\lambda^* \approx 67$. This configuration successfully reproduced the heavy-tailed kurtosis and ARCH effect observed in SPY (Figure~\ref{fig:model_comparison}). A state resolution of $N=100$ was selected to balance distributional fidelity and statistical robustness. Increasing $N$ improves regime granularity but is bounded above by data sparsity: at $N=350$, certain states are never visited in the historical record, leaving zero transition probabilities that break the ergodic structure of the chain. Conversely, at $N=10$ the model is too coarse to capture the heavy tails of the empirical distribution.

We performed Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) and Anderson-Darling (AD) tests to characterize the statistical consistency of simulated excess growth rate distributions against the historical data \cite{kolmogorov_1933, smirnov_1948, anderson_darling_1952}. The KS test measures the maximum deviation between empirical CDFs, while the AD test places greater weight on the distributional tails and is therefore a more sensitive diagnostic for the fat-tail properties of interest here.

In-sample results (Table~\ref{tab:model_comparison}) reveal a sharp contrast between model families. GARCH(1,1) achieved KS and AD pass rates of only 6.0\% and 1.4\%, respectively, indicating that its simulated marginal distribution is inconsistent with the observed data despite its well-known volatility clustering properties. HMM-NJ achieved KS and AD pass rates of 100.0\% and 97.0\%, and HMM-WJ achieved 98.5\% and 94.6\%. The ACF-MAE over lags 1--252 was 0.030 for GARCH, 0.059 for HMM-NJ, and 0.055 for HMM-WJ. The lower ACF-MAE for GARCH reflects that the GARCH variance equation is explicitly designed to match autocorrelation structure, yet this comes at the cost of distributional fidelity. The standard no-jumps model passed distributional tests but failed to reproduce volatility clustering, with the ACF of absolute growth rates decaying rapidly toward zero rather than exhibiting the empirically observed slow decay (Figure~\ref{fig:model_comparison}, panel~b). The hybrid model reproduced both the marginal distribution and the ARCH effect simultaneously.

Out-of-sample results over 249 trading days (January~2--December~31, 2025) show that all three models generalise, though with different patterns. GARCH achieved KS and AD pass rates of 78.1\% and 73.1\%. HMM-NJ achieved 96.6\% and 96.7\%, and HMM-WJ achieved 94.0\% and 94.2\% (Table~\ref{tab:model_comparison}). The simulated kurtosis for GARCH collapsed to 1.6 out-of-sample against an observed value of 6.9, confirming that GARCH does not preserve tail behaviour outside the training window. The HMM variants maintained simulated kurtosis near 5.0--5.1, a closer match to the observed 6.9. Volatility clustering persisted in the HMM-WJ out-of-sample paths, with the Poisson jump mechanism sustaining tail-state dwell times that the standard transition matrix cannot reproduce (Figure~\ref{fig:statistical_validation}). These results validate the hybrid framework as a generative tool for producing synthetic time series that remain statistically consistent with the underlying market dynamics across both in-sample and out-of-sample windows \cite{cont_empirical_2001, ryden_stylized_1998, bulla_stylized_2006}.

The correlation-distance distribution for the out-of-sample simulated paths was centred at a mean distance of $1-\rho \approx 1.0$, confirming that the generative framework produces temporally independent realisations that do not attempt to predict specific historical events \cite{fama_efficient_1970, assefa_generating_2021, kwon_can_2024}. This distinguishes the framework from a forecasting tool and positions it appropriately as an instrument for stress testing and scenario generation (Figure~\ref{fig:statistical_validation}).
