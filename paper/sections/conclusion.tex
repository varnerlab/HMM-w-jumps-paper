This study developed and validated a hybrid hidden Markov model (HMM) framework for generating synthetic equity excess growth rate paths that are statistically consistent with the three core stylized facts of financial time series: leptokurtic marginal distributions, the absence of linear autocorrelation in raw returns, and persistent volatility clustering. We summarize the contributions below.

\textbf{Hybrid HMM with Poisson jump-duration mechanism.}
The central methodological contribution is the augmentation of a discrete-state regime-switching model with a Poisson-driven jump-duration mechanism. Standard Markov transition logic underproduces volatility persistence because empirically estimated transition matrices assign negligible probability to transitions from central states into tail states, and even the tail states themselves exit within one to two steps on average under pure Markovian dynamics. By introducing a jump probability $\epsilon$ and mean jump duration $\lambda$ drawn from a Poisson distribution, the framework overrides standard transitions during periods of market stress and forces the model to dwell in high-volatility regimes for realistic durations. This mechanism is minimal in parameterization---requiring only two scalar hyperparameters---yet produces a qualitative shift in the temporal correlation structure of absolute returns, as confirmed by ACF comparison across lags up to 252 trading days.

\textbf{Empirical, non-iterative parameter estimation.}
A second contribution is the avoidance of the expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm in favor of direct frequentist transition counting. In the standard HMM setting, EM is used because latent state assignments are unobserved. Here, the quantile-based state partition explicitly assigns each historical observation to a hidden state, making direct counting possible. This simplification reduces computational cost, eliminates convergence sensitivity to initialization, and produces a model that is transparent and reproducible---properties that matter for large-scale pipelines operating over 424 assets.

\textbf{In-sample and out-of-sample validation.}
Using SPY (2014--2024) as a case study, the hybrid model achieved in-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Anderson-Darling pass rates exceeding 98 percent across 1,000 simulated paths. Out-of-sample testing on 220 trading days from 2025 showed pass rates of 83 percent and 80 percent, respectively, despite a 32 percent volatility discrepancy driven by elevated macroeconomic uncertainty not present in the training window. The correlation-distance analysis further confirmed that simulated paths are temporally independent of the historical trajectory, consistent with the efficient markets hypothesis and the requirements of a generative---rather than predictive---framework.

\textbf{Single-Index Factor Model extension for multi-asset generalization.}
The framework extends naturally to the full 424-asset universe via a Single-Index Model (SIM) factor structure. By regressing each asset's excess growth rate on the SPY factor and estimating idiosyncratic residual volatility, synthetic correlated multi-asset paths can be generated in a single pass: the hybrid HMM produces a common-factor path, and asset-level paths are recovered by projecting through asset-specific loadings. This construction preserves the regime-switching and jump-diffusion dynamics of the index while maintaining the cross-sectional correlation structure, enabling portfolio-level stress testing without fitting a high-dimensional multivariate model.

\subsection*{Broader implications}
The proposed framework offers practical utility for risk management and scenario design. Generative models that reproduce the stylized facts of financial markets enable stress testing under market regimes that are statistically plausible but not historically observed, addressing a key limitation of scenario libraries derived solely from empirical records. The interpretable regime structure---where each hidden state corresponds to a quantile-defined segment of the excess growth rate distribution---facilitates communication between quantitative analysts and risk managers, as states can be labeled (e.g., \textit{crash}, \textit{bear}, \textit{neutral}, \textit{bull}, \textit{rally}) and linked to economic narratives. The model's computational efficiency further supports Monte Carlo--based risk metrics such as Value-at-Risk and Conditional Value-at-Risk at scale across large asset universes.

\subsection*{Limitations}
Several limitations inform the interpretation of these results. First, the model assumes stationarity of the transition matrix across the full in-sample window; structural breaks in market microstructure---such as those accompanying regulatory changes or the COVID-19 shock---may not be adequately captured by a single static transition matrix. Second, the quantile-based state partition is fixed at estimation time and does not adapt to evolving volatility regimes; the boundaries are calibrated on the full in-sample distribution and may overweight the central mass relative to the tails in periods of prolonged market stress. Third, the SIM factor structure imposes a linear, single-factor model of cross-sectional dependence; this may understate tail co-movement during systemic events where correlations rise sharply and the Gaussian idiosyncratic shock assumption fails. Finally, the out-of-sample evaluation covers a single 220-day window, which limits the statistical power of out-of-sample inference; a longer or rolling out-of-sample evaluation would provide a more robust assessment.

\subsection*{Future work}
Several extensions follow naturally from the current framework. Time-varying transition matrices---estimated via rolling windows or Bayesian online learning---would allow the model to adapt to structural shifts in regime persistence. Multivariate HMMs, in which the hidden state jointly governs a small number of correlated assets, could capture higher-order dependencies beyond the single-factor SIM approximation and are particularly relevant for sector-level stress testing. Adaptive state definitions, such as non-parametric density-based clustering of excess growth rates, could replace the fixed Laplace quantile partition and improve tail coverage during crisis periods. Alternative factor structures, including the Fama-French three-factor model \cite{fama_french_1993}, could enrich the SIM extension by decomposing idiosyncratic risk further across size and value dimensions. Finally, integrating the hybrid HMM as the generative component within a larger portfolio optimization loop---where the model produces synthetic scenarios used to estimate tail-risk objectives---would provide an end-to-end computational finance application of the framework.
