In this study, we developed a variance-corrected method for composing
per-asset generators through a single-index model. The correction
removed the variance inflation caused by inserting a full-return
generator draw into the market factor. Across $423$ non-market assets,
the median absolute factor-loading error was $0.018$. Its exceedance
rate in the per-ticker one-day $99\%$ Value-at-Risk check was $0.95\%$,
compared with $0.99$--$1.09\%$ for the residual-fit methods. Its median
excess kurtosis and Hill index were $7.2$ and $0.365$.
Residual models fitted directly to the OLS residual series had KS
pass rates of $67$--$76\%$, compared with $50\%$ for the corrected
method, but required a separate fit for each asset.

The per-asset component was a hidden Markov marginal
model with Laplace-quantile states, Student-$t$ emissions, and direct
transition counting. On SPY, its jump-duration variant reduced
absolute-return autocorrelation error from $0.059$ to $0.052$
relative to the no-jump variant; its KS and AD pass rates were $97.6\%$
and $91.3\%$. GARCH had a lower autocorrelation error of $0.031$.
The single-asset result was
therefore a measured marginal--temporal tradeoff rather than a claim
of uniform superiority.

The current multi-asset implementation does not model cross-asset
residual covariance. In a $20$-asset allocator, the synthetic median
daily-rebalanced return exceeded the realized $2025$ return by about
$22$ percentage points. The static-to-daily-rebalance change accounted
for $27.73$ percentage points, while adding the allocation signal
changed the median by $0.13$ percentage points. A location-shift
correction set to a documented long-run prior adjusted the return
level without restoring the missing covariance. A full joint
residual model is needed before using these paths for
portfolio-level return forecasts.
